# Relativistic Doppler Effect
The **relativistic doppler** effect describes how the properties of light can be changed when the source of the light and the [observer](/pages/reference-frame "Reference Frame") of the light are moving in [relation](/pages/classical-relativity "Classical Relativity") to each other at speeds where [relativistic effects](/pages/special-relativity "Special Relativity") occur. It is effectively just the [classical doppler effect](/pages/classical-doppler-effect "Classical Doppler Effect"), but involving [special relativity](/pages/special-relativity "Special Relativity"), mainly [time dilation](/pages/time-dilation "Time Dilation").

A good starting point to understanding the **relativistic doppler effect** is to learn how the [classical doppler effect](/pages/classical-doppler-effect "Classical Doppler Effect") works.
