# Relativistic Doppler Effect
## What is the Relativistic Doppler Effect
The **relativistic Doppler** effect describes how light changes. When the source
of the light and the [observer](/pages/reference-frame "Reference Frame") of the
light are moving differently to each other at very fast speeds (where
[relativistic effects](/pages/special-relativity "Special Relativity") happen),
the colour of the light appears to change.

## Understanding the Relativistic Doppler Effect
To understand the **relativistic Doppler effect** you should first learn about
the [classical Doppler effect](/pages/classical-doppler-effect "Classical
Doppler Effect") and [time dilation](/pages/time-dilation "Time Dilation"). Read
those pages, then come back here.

The **relativistic Doppler effect** causes [Waves](/pages/waves "Waves") to be
squashed together and stretched apart. This changes the colour of the light,
which is the frequency of the wave. This change is similar to how the [classical
Doppler effect](/pages/classical-doppler-effect "Classical Doppler Effect")
changes other types of waves, such as sound waves. The thing that makes the
relativistic doppler effect different is that the change in the wave is due to
[time dilation](/pages/time-dilation "Time Dilation"). In some cases it is also
caused by a changing distance between you and the thing you are looking at
([classical Doppler effect](/pages/classical-doppler-effect "Classical Doppler
Effect")).

## Types of Relativistic Doppler Effect
There are two types of relativistic Doppler effect. Transverse and longitudal.
Consider the two cases in the diagram below. Number one is a satellite which is
an example of the transverse effect. The satellite orbits the earth and
communicates with us using light waves. Number two is a spaceship which is an
example of the longitudal effect. The spaceship is flying away from the Earth,
and is sending light waves back to the Earth.

![Diagram of space with the Earth orbited by a satellite and a space shuttle flying away from the Earth](/space.png "Space Diagram
")

### Transverse
In this case, the satellite orbits the Earth. This means that its distance from
the Earth doesn't change. Because it isn't moving closer or further away, the
[classical Doppler effect](/pages/classical-doppler-effect "Classical Doppler
Effect") doesn't apply. But, the wave signals that we recieve from satellites
still appear stretched out. Because the satellite is moving much faster than us,
we see its time slow down due to [time dilation](/pages/time-dilation "Time
Dilation"). This means that we watch it produce its waves more slowly. So less
waves reach us each second, which means the frequency of the wave decreases.

### Longitudal
In this case, the ship is moving away from the Earth at a very high speed. This
means that both time dilation and the classical Doppler effect apply to it.
Similarly to the satellite, the speed of the ship means that its time appears
to slow down. This leads to us seeing the frequency of its waves as lower
because the waves are being produced by the ship more slowly. But because the
distance between the Earth and the ship is changing, the classical doppler
effect also changes the wave. Because we are behind the spaceship, just like the
car, the gaps in the waves gets bigger. This means that the frequency becomes
lower. Both of these effects add to each other to decrease the frequency of the
wave even more.

## What does this mean?
The relativistic Doppler effect explains that the universe is expanding. This is because astronomers have observed [redshifting](/pages/redshift "Redshift").
