## What is the Doppler Effect
The classical **Doppler effect** is when the thing making a wave (the source)
moves compared to the thing that sees or hears it (its
[observer](/pages/reference-frame "Reference Frame")). This changes the
[properties of the wave](/pages/waves "Waves"). In simple terms, a moving object
can make different waves to an object that is standing still.

## Understanding the Doppler Effect
First you need to understand how waves move. Waves do not copy any movement from
the thing that created them. They start at the point that they were made and
ripple outwards. They are always the same, whether their source was moving or
not. You can see this in the simulation below. Click to make a wave. It doesn't
matter how fast your mouse cursor is moving, because the wave always starts from
the point that you clicked on and spreads out in every direction at the same
speed.

{{< wave-sim >}}

In the next simulation, a car is making a sound all the time. Drag the car
around. Each wave starts from the spot that the car was when it made that wave,
just like when you were clicking the mouse. Look at the cirles in front of the
car, the lines are closer together. Behind the car, they are further apart.

{{< car-wave-sim >}}

This is the **Doppler effect**. As the car moves, it makes each new wave close
to the edge of the last one. This squashes the waves together at the front.
being much closer on that side. The lines show the peaks (highest points) of the
waves. A shorter gap between the lines means a shorter wavelength. Because of
what we know about [properties of waves](/pages/waves "Waves"), this shorter
wavelength leads to a higher frequency. So if you are in front of the car you
will experience a higher frequency wave, which would be a higher pitched sound.
Likewise, if you are behind the car, the waves will stretch out  you will experience a lower frequency wave
which would be a lower pitched sound. Imagine the 2D graph of the wave being
stretched out or squashed depending on whether you are in front of or behind the
source of the wave when it is moving.

![Comparison of sound waves as a car gets closer and further](/wave.png "Wave")

You have probably heard the **Doppler effect** when a car drives past you. The
sound gets higher pitched as the car comes towards you, then goes lower as it
goes past. When the car is coming towards you, the gaps between the waves are
small. When the car passes, you are suddenly behind it, so the gaps get bigger.
You can hear this change in the video. The F1 car goes past very fast, so you go
from being in front of it to behind it very quickly. That is why the difference
in pitch of the car is so easy to hear.
