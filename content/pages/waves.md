## What is a Wave
Waves are ripples that move energy through space or matter (stuff). Waves can be
many things and take many forms. Examples of waves are:
* Waves in the ocean
* Sound waves which make up the noises you hear
* Light can be observed as a wave and has the properties of one

## Properties of Waves
Waves have three main properties:
* Frequency
* Wavelength
* Amplitude

### Frequency
Frequency is the number of up and down cycles of the wave at a point, per
second.

Here's what frequency means for each example:
* How many waves hit the beach each second
* The frequency of sound waves changes its pitch. \
  A higher frequency sounds higher pitched and a lower frequency sounds lower
  pitched.
* The frequency of light changes its colour. \
  if the frequency is too low or too high, it becomes invisible to us. A certain
  range of frequencies make up the rainbow. \
  Red light is a lower frequency than blue light.

### Wavelength
Frequency is tied to wavelength. The wavelength is the distance between two
matching points on the wave, usually from one peak to the next.

If the wavelength is shorter, the frequency increases because the waves are
closer together. \
Closer waves mean more waves happen each second. For example, if the waves in
the ocean all got closer together, more waves hit the beach each second, which
means the frequency would increase.

### Amplitude
Amplitude is the height of the wave.

A higher amplitude means that the wave has more energy. Amplitude can also
increase when waves overlap and add to one another. \
Like when you get double bounced on a trampoline.

Amplitude is
also a part of the earlier examples:
* The amplitude of a wave at the beach is its height. The bigger the wave, the
  harder it will hit you and might knock you over. \
  This is because the bigger waves carry more energy.

* The amplitude of a sound wave is how loud it sounds. \
  Something that makes a louder noise makes waves with more energy (higher
  amplitude) which sounds louder.
* Induvidual light particles (photons) always have the same amplitude. \
  But when many photons arrive together, they add up. \
  This makes the amplitude (brightness) of the light appear to increase. Even
  though each photon didn't change.

These properties of waves can be changed by the Doppler effect.
