## What is a Wave
Waves are ripples that move energy through space or matter (stuff). Waves can be
many things and take many forms. Examples of waves are:
* Waves in the ocean
* Sound waves which make up every noise you hear
* Light can be observed as a wave and has the properties of one

## Properties of Waves
Waves have three main properties:
* Frequency
* Wavelength
* Amplitude

![](/wave-properties.jpg "Wave properties")

### Frequency
Frequency is the number of times that the wave goes up and down at a point per
second. Frequency can be applied to the examples from earlier in the following
ways:
* How many waves that hit the beach each second
* The frequency of sound waves changes the pitch that you hear them, a higher
  frequency sounds higher pitched and a lower frequency sounds lower pitched.
* The frequency of light changes the colour, it can be invisible if the
  frequency is too low or too high, but a specific range of frequencies makes up
  the rainbow. Red light is a lower frequency than blue light.

### Wavelength
Frequency is tied to wavelength. The wavelength is the gap between identical
points on the wave, usually the peaks. If the wavelength is shorter, the
frequency increases because the waves are closer together. Closer waves mean
more waves happen each second. For example, if the waves in the ocean all got
closer together, more waves hit the beach each second, which means the frequency
would increase.

### Amplitude
Amplitude is the height of the wave. A higher amplitude means that the wave has
more energy, or that there are more waves happening at the same time which add
to one another (like when you get double bounced on a trampoline). Amplitude is
also a part of the earlier examples:
* The amplitude of a wave at the beach is the height of the wave, the bigger the
  wave the harder it will hit you and knock you over. This is because the bigger
  waves carry more energy.
* Similarly to this, the amplitude of a sound wave is just how loud it is.
  Something that makes a louder noise makes waves with more energy (higher
  amplitude) which sounds louder.
* Light technically doesn't have amplitude, because each photon (particle of
  light) has the same amplitude. However multiple photons can add up with one
  another to effectively increase the amplitude, even though each photon is the
  same. This is observed as an increase in brightness.
